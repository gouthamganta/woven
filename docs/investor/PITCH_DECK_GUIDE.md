# Pitch Deck Guide

This guide describes the content for each slide in a Woven investor pitch deck. Each section lists what to include — not the final slide copy. All claims should be sourced from facts in this documentation set. Where market data is included, note that figures are widely reported market estimates, not proprietary data.

---

## Slide 1 — The Problem

Frame the problem as experienced by users, not as a market gap. The goal is emotional recognition before intellectual argument.

- **Swipe fatigue is documented, not anecdotal.** Multiple widely reported consumer surveys show the majority of dating app users describe their experience as exhausting, frustrating, or anxiety-producing. Include 1-2 cited survey references if available; if not, describe the pattern without false precision.
- **The ghost problem.** The standard post-match experience: match forms, one or both users sends an opener, conversation stalls or is never started, the match disappears. This is not a user failure — it is a design failure. Apps that treat matching as the end state create a product that optimizes for match count, not connection outcomes.
- **The stated preference fallacy.** People say they want X in a partner and consistently choose Y. Questionnaire-based matching has a structural ceiling — it is bounded by users' self-knowledge, which is limited and often inaccurate. This is the core mechanism failure of the dominant apps.
- **The visibility problem.** Showing compatibility scores, like counts, and desirability signals turns dating into a performance. Users optimize for platform metrics rather than for genuine self-presentation. The mechanism meant to help them find a partner begins working against that goal.
- **One clear statement:** The problem is not that there aren't enough people on dating apps. The problem is that the apps aren't working.

## Slide 2 — The Solution

Describe Woven's approach at the level of principles first, then mechanics. Avoid feature lists on this slide.

- **Behavioral matching, not stated preference matching.** ECHO learns from what users do — how quickly they respond, whether they send voice notes, whether they agree to meet — not from what they say they want. The signal is revealed behavior.
- **Invisible AI.** No compatibility scores, no AI labels, no community ratings. Users experience better matches; they do not see a dashboard of how they are being evaluated. AI is infrastructure, not a feature.
- **Intentional UX by design.** The finite daily deck (Moments), the spark economy, and the trial period are not features added on top of a swipe engine. They are the structural choices that make the behavioral signal high-quality. Intentional users generate better training data.
- **Full-journey product.** Woven does not end at match formation. The trial period, Find Love, and AI-generated date ideas extend the product experience to the point where users need help moving from conversation to meeting.
- **One clear statement:** Woven is what dating apps look like when they are optimized for your outcome, not for their engagement metrics.

## Slide 3 — How It Works

Walk through the user journey as a narrative, not a feature list. The goal is comprehension, not completeness.

- **Onboarding → Moments (daily deck).** User completes onboarding. Each day, ECHO generates a curated deck of candidates (Moments). Not infinite scroll — a finite, considered set. User makes choices (Magical = ◈, Resonant = ◇) and writes a short note explaining each choice. The note is background signal, never shown to the other person.
- **Balloon → Trial period.** A mutual choice creates a Balloon (connection window). When both users open the chat, a 3-minute mutual trial window begins. Both decide: CONTINUE, END (with reason), or BLOCK. This is the commitment checkpoint.
- **Trial accepted → Find Love.** If both users continue, the full connection unlocks. Five minutes after first messages are exchanged, Find Love activates: 3 personalized date ideas generated by ECHO, tailored to both users' behavioral history and the tone that preceded their fastest first messages.
- **Match flow enrichment.** Within the match, users can play KnowMeAgent (preference discovery game) and RedGreenFlagAgent (values alignment game). Voice notes are supported. Every interaction generates behavioral signal that feeds ECHO.
- **Spark economy.** 5 sparks per day. Each Drawn action (acting on a candidate who has liked you) costs 1 spark. If a match ends with no messages exchanged, 0.5 sparks are refunded. No hard paywall — engagement is the gate.

## Slide 4 — ECHO: The Technical Moat

This slide should explain the learning flywheel, not list technical specs. The goal is to convey that Woven's matching engine improves with use and is not trivially replicable.

- **The flywheel description:** More intentional user behavior → richer behavioral signals → more accurate ConnectionScore outcome labels → better per-user weight adaptation → more accurate daily deck → more intentional user behavior. This is the compounding advantage. A competing app starting today would need years of behavioral data to replicate the weight adaptation accuracy that accumulates in Woven's pipeline.
- **ConnectionScore: what ECHO is learning.** The outcome label is not "did they match" — it is a 7-signal composite: BalloonPopped (0.05), TrialRequested (0.10), TrialAccepted (0.25), ConversationDepth (0.20), DateAccepted (0.15), ExplicitFeedback (0.15), LoveReactions (0.10). A user who matched, entered the trial, continued, had a long conversation, and accepted a date idea generates a high ConnectionScore label. This label trains per-user weights.
- **Per-user adaptation.** ECHO does not apply the same weights to every user. Weekly logistic regression (L2 regularized) adjusts the 16 scoring component weights per user based on their accumulated ConnectionScore labels. A user whose connections are predicted by voice resonance gets a higher voice weight. A user whose connections are predicted by lifestyle embedding similarity gets a higher lifestyle weight.
- **16 components, 9 modalities.** Briefly: ECHO evaluates 16 dimensions of predicted compatibility. Nine of those are driven by embedding similarity across purpose-specific vector types — including a 192-dim voice embedding generated by SpeechBrain ECAPA-TDNN, a component no competitor currently includes.
- **Not replicable without the data.** The architecture can be described; replicating it requires the behavioral signal ledger that accumulates from intentional user engagement. That ledger is Woven's data moat.

## Slide 5 — Infrastructure

Frame this slide as "production-ready, not prototype." The goal is to establish technical credibility without a technical deep-dive.

- **Production-deployed on Azure.** Four container pods (frontend, backend, workers, SpeechBrain) running on Azure Container Apps. API and batch workers are isolated: batch jobs run in the workers pod on off-peak schedules (03:50-04:00 UTC), preventing compute contention with request-serving.
- **Data layer.** PostgreSQL Flexible Server with pgvector extension and HNSW indexes for fast vector similarity search. Redis Standard C1 (99.9% SLA) for hot-path caching. Azure Service Bus Standard for durable async processing with dead-letter queue and max 5 retries.
- **Media and security.** Azure Blob Storage with private containers (profile-photos, tile-media, voice-notes). SAS token access — no public URLs. AES-256-GCM encryption on all PII fields (email, full name, city, state, reflection sentences) at the application layer.
- **Infrastructure as code.** All resources defined in Terraform. OIDC-based CI/CD — no secrets in Terraform state, no long-lived credentials in CI/CD. Repeatable environment creation.
- **AI cost controls.** Daily budget cap ($50 current), CircuitBreakerService for graceful degradation when budget is exhausted, gpt-4.1-mini for all generation tasks. SpeechBrain runs self-hosted (no per-call API cost for voice embeddings).

## Slide 6 — Market

Be explicit that all figures are widely reported market estimates. Do not present them as proprietary research.

- **TAM:** The global dating app market is widely reported to exceed $10 billion in annual revenue. Include source reference if available.
- **Market concentration:** The top 3 platforms (Tinder, Hinge, Bumble) account for the majority of global market share. This concentration defines the interaction paradigm that Woven is designed as an alternative to.
- **The behavioral shift:** Consumer surveys widely document growing frustration with swipe-based apps among the 22-35 demographic. "Swipe fatigue" has become a recognized consumer sentiment category. This is the market signal that makes the premium-intentional quadrant commercially meaningful.
- **SAM/SOM framing:** Woven is not targeting all dating app users. It is targeting intentional daters — users willing to invest effort in exchange for better matches. This is a definable, reachable subset. Do not invent a precise percentage; describe the behavioral characteristics that define the addressable segment (see TARGET_USERS.md).
- **Note for presenter:** These are market-context figures, not Woven proprietary data. Present them as context for the opportunity, not as the foundation of a financial model.

## Slide 8 — Business Model

Be honest about what is built vs. what is planned.

- **Current model — spark economy (built).** 5 sparks per day free, wallet max 10. Each Drawn action costs 1 spark. The soft gate rewards engagement and filters for intentional users. This is not a revenue model yet — it is a behavioral filter. The economy is designed to make sense before monetization is layered on.
- **Premium path (not yet built).** The spark economy creates a natural premium surface: additional sparks, higher daily deck count, priority in deck ordering. This is the intended monetization path. It is not built. Do not present it as a current revenue stream.
- **Data moat.** The behavioral signal ledger (`MatchSignalLogs`) accumulates with every user interaction. This ledger is the training data source for ECHO weight learning. As the user base grows, the ledger grows, and per-user weight adaptation becomes more accurate. This is a compounding data asset — the longer Woven operates, the harder its matching quality is to replicate.
- **No advertising model.** Woven does not serve ads and has not designed the product to support an advertising revenue model. The absence of advertising is itself a positioning statement for the target user segment.
- **Honest framing:** Revenue model is designed and architecturally ready to implement. It has not been built or activated. This is a pre-revenue product seeking investment to reach the user scale at which behavioral data makes the matching engine demonstrably superior.

## Slide 9 — Known Gaps and Roadmap

Show this slide. Not showing it creates a credibility problem when investors find the gaps in due diligence. Showing it demonstrates roadmap awareness and honest self-assessment.

- **Push notifications (not built).** Service worker, VAPID, and Web Push endpoint are not yet implemented. Users receive no out-of-app notifications. This is a known retention mechanism gap — push notifications are one of the primary re-engagement tools in consumer apps. It is the highest-priority product gap.
- **CfScore batch worker (not wired).** `CollaborativeFilteringService` exists. No batch worker runs it. The `cf` component in ECHO (base weight=0.03) currently returns a default value. Enabling this component requires provisioning the batch job.
- **SharedTileAffinity component (depends on CfScore).** The `shared_tile_affinity` scoring component (base weight=0.05) depends on CfScore data. Enabling it requires the CfScore batch worker to be running first.
- **PreferenceEmbedding from ChatNotes (partial).** The worker stub exists; the full wiring from ChatNote content to embedding generation and storage is in progress.
- **"Your Turn" chat list indicator and active/online indicator (not built).** Designed, not implemented. These are retention and engagement features, not core matching features.
- **Roadmap framing:** These are scoped gaps, not unknown unknowns. The infrastructure to address each one exists. The build order is: push notifications → CfScore worker → SharedTileAffinity → PreferenceEmbedding wiring. Each closes a specific gap in either retention (push), matching accuracy (CfScore, SharedTileAffinity), or signal quality (PreferenceEmbedding).
